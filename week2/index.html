Formula Sheet • Reference Card

Master Formula Sheet: Power Supply & Regulator Calculations

Structured Mathematical Equations for AC Rectification, Smoothing & Zener Attenuation

⚡ 1. Peak & DC Voltages

• AC Input Peak: $$V_{s(peak)} = \sqrt{2} \cdot V_{rms}$$ • Bridge Peak Output: $$V_m = V_{s(peak)} - 2V_D = \sqrt{2}V_{rms} - 1.4\text{ V}$$ • Average Input DC: $$V_{dc(in)} = V_m - \frac{V_{r(in), p-p}}{2}$$ • Ripple Minimum Trough: $$V_{in(min)} = V_m - V_{r(in), p-p}$$

🧮 2. Resistor & Capacitor Sizing

• Load Current: $$I_L = \frac{V_Z}{R_L}$$ • Max Series Resistor: $$R_{S(max)} = \frac{V_{in(min)} - V_Z}{I_{L(max)} + I_{Z(min)}}$$ • Filter Capacitor: $$C \ge \frac{I_{in}}{2 f \cdot V_{r(in), p-p}} \quad \left(I_{in} = \frac{V_{dc(in)} - V_Z}{R_S}\right)$$

📉 3. Output Ripple Attenuation

• Output Ripple: $$V_{r(out), p-p} \approx V_{r(in), p-p} \cdot \left( \frac{r_z}{R_S + r_z} \right)$$ • Regulation Boundary Check: $$V_{in(min)} > V_Z + I_{in(min)} R_S$$ • Ensure $I_Z \ge I_{ZK}$ ($1\text{ mA}$) at the lowest trough.

🔥 4. Power Dissipation Checks

• Resistor Power: $$P_{RS} = \frac{(V_m - V_Z)^2}{R_S} \quad (\text{Select } 0.25\text{W or } 0.5\text{W})$$ • Max Zener Power (at $I_{L(min)}$): $$P_Z = V_Z \cdot (I_{in} - I_{L(min)}) \quad (\le 1.0\text{ W for 1N47xx})$$
Engineering Glossary • Definitions of Terms

Glossary of Mathematical Terms & Circuit Variables

Clear Step-by-Step Definitions of Quantities and Symbols Used in Power Calculations

$V_{rms}$ (RMS Voltage) Volts [V]
Root-Mean-Square value of alternating voltage. The equivalent DC voltage producing identical resistive heating ($9.0\text{ V RMS}$).
$V_{s(peak)}$ / $V_m$ (Peak Voltage) Volts [V]
Maximum instantaneous crest voltage. $V_{s(peak)} = \sqrt{2} \cdot V_{rms} \approx 12.73\text{ V}$. Bridge peak $V_m = V_{s(peak)} - 2V_D \approx 11.33\text{ V}$.
$V_{r(in), p-p}$ (Input Filter Ripple) Volts peak-to-peak [$V_{pp}$]
The peak-to-peak ripple across filter capacitor $C$ before the series resistor ($V_{r(in), p-p} \le 1.7\text{ V}$).
$V_{dc(in)}$ & $V_{in(min)}$ Volts [V]
$V_{dc(in)}$: Average DC voltage on capacitor ($V_m - \frac{V_r}{2} = 10.48\text{ V}$).
$V_{in(min)}$: Lowest ripple trough voltage ($V_m - V_r = 9.63\text{ V}$).
$V_Z$ & $I_{ZK}$ (Zener Voltage & Knee Current) V & mA
$V_Z$: Regulated breakdown voltage ($5.1\text{ V}$ 1N4733A).
$I_{ZK}$: Minimum knee current required to sustain breakdown ($1.0\text{ mA}$).
$r_z$ / $Z_{ZT}$ (Dynamic Zener Impedance) Ohms [Ω]
Internal AC slope resistance of the Zener in breakdown ($r_z \approx 7\ \Omega$). Forms the AC divider attenuating output ripple.
$R_S$ & $I_{in}$ (Series Resistor & Current) kΩ & mA
$R_S$: Series current-limiting resistor ($1.5\text{ k}\Omega$).
$I_{in}$ ($I_S$): Total current supplied by filter capacitor ($I_S = I_Z + I_L \approx 3.82\text{ mA}$).
$V_{r(out), p-p}$ (Regulated Output Ripple) Millivolts [$mV_{pp}$]
Attenuated AC ripple remaining on the regulated DC output across the load ($V_{r(out), p-p} \approx 4.64\text{ mV}_{p-p}$).

AC Fundamentals • Measurement Science

AC Waveform Parameters: Amplitude, Peak, Peak-to-Peak & RMS

Understanding Essential Voltage Definitions on the Oscilloscope and DMM

Sine Wave Parameters: $V_{pk}$ (Peak), $V_{pp}$ (Peak-to-Peak), and $V_{rms}$ (Root-Mean-Square).
📐 Key Waveform Mathematical Definitions
1. Peak Voltage ($V_{pk}$ or $V_{max}$):
Maximum potential measured from the zero-volt baseline to the crest: $$V_{pk} = V_{rms} \times \sqrt{2} \approx 1.414 \times V_{rms}$$
2. Peak-to-Peak Voltage ($V_{pk-pk}$ or $V_{pp}$):
Total vertical excursion from negative trough to positive crest: $$V_{pp} = 2 \times V_{pk}$$
3. RMS Voltage ($V_{rms}$ — Root Mean Square):
The equivalent DC voltage that produces identical heating power in a resistive load: $$V_{rms} = \frac{V_{pk}}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.707 \times V_{pk}$$
4. Amplitude:
The magnitude of change from the center equilibrium baseline to peak ($= V_{pk}$ for un-offset AC).
⚡ Lab Tip: Multimeters on AC range measure RMS ($9.00\text{ V RMS}$), while Oscilloscopes display Peak-to-Peak ($25.4\text{ V}_{pp}$).
System Architecture • Power Electronics

System Block Diagram: Regulated DC Power Supply

Stage-by-Stage Energy Transformation from High-Voltage AC to Clamped DC

Complete 5-Stage Power Conversion Chain
1. Step-Down Transformer
Steps lethal mains ($230\text{ V RMS}$) down to safe low-voltage AC ($9.0\text{ V RMS}, 50\text{ Hz}$) and provides galvanic isolation.
2. Full-Wave Bridge Rectifier
4 diodes ($D_1\text{--}D_4$) invert negative AC half-cycles into unidirectional pulsating DC ($f_r = 100\text{ Hz}, V_m \approx 11.33\text{ V}$).
3. Reservoir Smoothing Filter
Electrolytic capacitor ($C_1 = 47\ \mu\text{F} / 100\ \mu\text{F}$) charges on peaks and discharges into the load, reducing ripple ($V_{r(in)} \le 1.7\text{ V}$).
4. Zener Voltage Regulator ($R_S + ZD_1$)
Series resistor $R_S$ ($1.5\text{ k}\Omega$) absorbs excess DC, while Zener diode clamps output to stable $5.1\text{ V}$ DC ($V_{r(out)} \approx 4.6\text{ mV}$).
5. Output Load ($R_L$)
Subsystem draws clean, constant DC current ($I_L = 0.23\text{ mA to } 1.09\text{ mA}$) without ripple degradation.

Stage 4: What is Zener Voltage Regulation? — Physics & Mechanics

Operating in Reverse Breakdown, Constant Potential Clamping & Resistor $R_s$ Sizing

Dual-Coding Analogy: Zener Diode operates like a hydraulic pressure relief valve clamping output potential.
⚡ How Zener Regulation Works

Unlike standard diodes which block reverse current until destructive breakdown, a Zener Diode is heavily doped to exploit controlled Avalanche / Zener Breakdown.

Key Regulation Mechanics:
• Connected in REVERSE BIAS (Cathode to positive rail).
• When $V_{in} > V_Z$, current enters the sharp breakdown knee.
• Across wide current shifts, voltage remains clamped at nominal $V_Z$.
• Series Resistor ($R_s$): Absorbs excess voltage ($V_{Rs} = V_{dc(in)} - V_Z$) and limits current: $$I_{in} = I_S = \frac{V_{dc(in)} - V_Z}{R_S} = I_Z + I_L$$
⚠️ Dropout Condition: If load current $I_L \ge I_S$, then $I_Z \to 0$, causing the Zener to exit breakdown and drop out of regulation!
Class Demonstration • 5.1V Prototype

Step-by-Step Design Procedure: 5.1 V Regulated Supply

Structured Component Calculation: $V_{rms} = 9\text{V}$, $V_Z = 5.1\text{V}$ (1N4733A), Max Ripple $\le 1.7\text{V}$

1️⃣ Load Currents ($I_L$)

• For $R_{L1} = 4.7\text{ k}\Omega$: $$I_{L1} = \frac{5.1\text{ V}}{4700\ \Omega} \approx 1.085\text{ mA} \quad (I_{L(max)})$$ • For $R_{L2} = 10\text{ k}\Omega$: $$I_{L2} = \frac{5.1\text{ V}}{10000\ \Omega} = 0.510\text{ mA}$$ • For $R_{L3} = 22\text{ k}\Omega$: $$I_{L3} = \frac{5.1\text{ V}}{22000\ \Omega} \approx 0.232\text{ mA} \quad (I_{L(min)})$$

2️⃣ Peak & Minimum DC Input

• Peak: $$V_m = \sqrt{2}(9\text{V}) - 1.4\text{V} \approx 12.73\text{V} - 1.4\text{V} = 11.33\text{ V}$$ • Average Input: $$V_{dc(in)} = V_m - \frac{V_{r(in), p-p}}{2} = 11.33\text{V} - 0.85\text{V} = 10.48\text{ V}$$ • Minimum Trough: $$V_{in(min)} = V_m - V_{r(in), p-p} = 11.33\text{V} - 1.7\text{V} = 9.63\text{ V}$$

3️⃣ Sizing Series Resistor ($R_S$)

• Choose safety margin: $I_{Z(min)} \ge 2 \cdot I_{ZK} \approx 2.0\text{ mA}$
• Max Resistance: $$R_{S(max)} = \frac{9.63\text{V} - 5.1\text{V}}{1.085\text{mA} + 2.0\text{mA}} = \frac{4.53\text{V}}{3.085\text{mA}} \approx 1468\ \Omega$$ • Standard Selection: $$\mathbf{R_S = 1.2\text{ k}\Omega \text{ or } 1.5\text{ k}\Omega \quad (1200\ \Omega \text{ optimal})}$$

4️⃣ Sizing Filter Capacitor ($C$)

• Total Current (with $R_S = 1.2\text{ k}\Omega$): $$I_{in} = \frac{10.48\text{V} - 5.1\text{V}}{1200\ \Omega} = \frac{5.38\text{V}}{1200\ \Omega} \approx 4.48\text{ mA}$$ • Minimum Capacitance: $$C \ge \frac{4.48 \times 10^{-3}\text{A}}{2 \cdot (50\text{Hz}) \cdot (1.7\text{V})} = \frac{4.48\text{ mA}}{170} \approx 26.4\ \mu\text{F}$$ • Standard Selection: $$\mathbf{C = 47\ \mu\text{F} \text{ or } 100\ \mu\text{F} \quad (\ge 25\text{V})}$$

📋 Output Ripple Attenuation ($V_{r(out), p-p}$)

Using $V_{r(in), p-p} = 1.7\text{ V}$, $R_S = 1200\ \Omega$, and $r_z \approx 7\ \Omega$: $$V_{r(out), p-p} \approx 1.7\text{ V} \cdot \left( \frac{7\ \Omega}{1200\ \Omega + 7\ \Omega} \right) \approx 9.86\text{ mV}_{p-p} \quad (\ll 100\text{ mV})$$ Power Ratings Verification:

Theory, Simulation & Reality: Engineering Discrepancy Analysis

Quantifying Percentage Difference and Root-Cause Engineering Explanations

📉 Why Doesn't Reality Match Calculation?

Real components exhibit physical tolerances and non-ideal characteristics:

  • 🔹 Diode Forward Drop Variation: Silicon diodes vary between $0.62\text{ V}$ and $0.85\text{ V}$ depending on instantaneous junction temperature and current density.
  • 🔹 Electrolytic Capacitor Tolerances: Standard capacitors have wide manufacturing tolerances (typically $\pm 20\%$), shifting measured filter ripple.
  • 🔹 Source Internal Resistance: Transformer and bench signal generators have non-zero source impedance ($R_{int}$), causing input voltage sag under load.
  • 🔹 Zener Dynamic Impedance ($r_z$): Zener breakdown curves have finite slope ($7\ \Omega$), so $V_Z$ varies slightly with current.
🧮 Percentage Difference Formula
Percentage Difference:
$$\%\text{ Difference} = \frac{|\text{Measured} - \text{Theoretical}|}{\text{Theoretical}} \times 100\%$$
Example: Theory = $5.10\text{ V}$, Measured = $5.04\text{ V}$:
$$\%\text{ Diff} = \frac{|5.04 - 5.10|}{5.10} \times 100\% = 1.18\% \quad (\text{Well within } 5\% \text{ tolerance})$$
