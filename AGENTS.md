# Unit 19 Learning Aim A – Weekly Lesson Resource Generation

## 1. PROJECT PURPOSE

This repository contains the complete teaching and learning resource pack for:

**Pearson BTEC Level 3 Engineering**
**Unit 19 – Electronic Devices and Circuits**
**Learning Aim A – Explore the safe operation and applications of analogue devices and circuits that form the building blocks of commercial circuits.**

The repository is used to generate, maintain and quality-assure weekly teaching resources.

The primary goal is:

> **Students learning to think and work like electronics engineers.**

The goal is NOT:

> Students completing a collection of worksheets.

Resources must therefore prioritise authentic engineering problems, practical investigation, measurement, troubleshooting, analysis and communication.

**Full-time Level 3 BTEC Engineering students.**

* workplace electronics experience
* professional engineering experience

* extensive practical troubleshooting experience

# 5. CORE TEACHING PHILOSOPHY

Use a combination of:

* Exploratory learning
* Problem-based learning
* Project/problem-based approaches
* Learning by doing
* Paired learning
* Group work where useful
* Practical investigation
* Engineering troubleshooting
* Reflection
* Evidence-based communication

The core practical culture is:

```text
BUILD → TEST → BREAK → FIX → REPEAT
```

The core reasoning cycle is:

```text
PREDICT → TEST → EXPLAIN
```

For circuit investigations use:

```text
PREDICT
   ↓
SIMULATE
   ↓
BUILD
   ↓
TEST
   ↓
COMPARE
   ↓
EXPLAIN
   ↓
DOCUMENT
```

Theory should normally be introduced because it is required to solve the current engineering problem.

Avoid long theory-only teaching sequences.

Do not introduce large amounts of theory before students have a reason to use it.

# 6. WORKPLACE NARRATIVE

Students operate throughout Learning Aim A as a developing:

> **Junior Electronics Engineering Team**

Every week must have a realistic workplace engineering context.

The lesson should feel like a genuine engineering task.

## Weekly roles and scenarios

### Week 1 – Junior Electronics Technician

**Scenario:** Prototype fault-finding

Students investigate a faulty electronic prototype, work safely, identify faults and use measurements to diagnose the problem.

### Week 2 – Junior Power Electronics Engineer

**Scenario:** Diode power/protection investigation

Students investigate diode behaviour and select/use diode circuits for rectification, protection and voltage control.

### Week 3 – Junior Control Engineer

**Scenario:** Transistor load-control interface

Students investigate transistor operation and use a transistor to control a real load/interface.

### Week 4 – Junior Instrumentation Engineer

**Scenario:** Op-amp sensor signal conditioning

Students investigate and construct op-amp circuits to condition a sensor signal.

### Week 5 – Prototype/Test Engineer

**Scenario:** Prototype verification

Students verify that an analogue prototype operates as intended using simulation and physical testing.

### Week 6 – Test/Verification Engineer

**Scenario:** Simulation versus physical results

Students investigate why theoretical, simulated and physical results differ.

### Week 7 – Junior Electronics Engineer / Design Review

**Scenario:** Product-development recommendation

Students review the evidence and make an engineering recommendation about whether the circuit/design should proceed.

# 7. AUTHENTIC ENGINEERING FIRST

Do not make lessons appear to exist simply to satisfy academic assessment requirements.

The sequence should be:

```text
ENGINEERING PROBLEM
        ↓
INVESTIGATE
        ↓
LEARN REQUIRED THEORY
        ↓
PREDICT
        ↓
SIMULATE
        ↓
BUILD
        ↓
TEST
        ↓
TROUBLESHOOT
        ↓
ANALYSE
        ↓
DOCUMENT
        ↓
REFLECT
```

The assignment is the formal communication of this engineering work.

It should not become the activity itself.

Avoid lesson structures such as:

> "Today we are going to complete A.P2."

Instead use:

> "The prototype needs to be verified. Build the circuit, measure its behaviour and determine whether it meets the engineering requirement."

Assessment criteria should influence the design of the learning and evidence, but should not dominate the student-facing narrative.

# 8. RETRIEVAL STRATEGY

Every detailed lesson plan MUST contain deliberate retrieval.

Use:

## Engineering Toolbox / Back to the Bench

Allow approximately:

**5–10 minutes at the start of the lesson.**

Retrieval should normally include a mixture of:

* Component/device recall
* Concept recall
* Calculation
* Fault-finding question
* Workplace application question

Retrieval must be cumulative.

## Cumulative retrieval progression

### Week 1

Diagnostic baseline.

### Week 2

Retrieve Week 1.

### Week 3

Retrieve Weeks 1–2.

### Week 4

Retrieve Weeks 1–3.

### Week 5

Retrieve Weeks 1–4.

### Week 6

Retrieve the complete technical block.

### Week 7

Retrieve the whole of Learning Aim A.

Do not treat retrieval as isolated weekly quizzes.

The intended model is:

```text
A1
 ↓
A1 + A2
 ↓
A1 + A2 + A3
 ↓
A1 + A2 + A3 + A4
 ↓
A1 + A2 + A3 + A4 + A5
 ↓
Whole technical block
 ↓
Whole Learning Aim A
```

# 9. RETRIEVAL BEFORE PRACTICAL WORK

Important practical activities should normally include a short retrieval/prediction checkpoint immediately beforehand.

For example:

```text
Retrieve diode behaviour
        ↓
Predict forward/reverse behaviour
        ↓
Build circuit
        ↓
Measure
        ↓
Explain result
```

Do not allow practical work to become "follow the instructions and copy the result."

Students should predict before measuring wherever practical.

# 10. ENGINEER EXIT TICKET

Every lesson must end with an:

> **Engineer Exit Ticket**

It must contain:

1. One question from today's learning.
2. One question from a recent lesson.
3. One cumulative question.

Where appropriate, include an engineering application or troubleshooting question.

Exit tickets should provide useful formative information to the tutor.

Avoid questions that only test recall when the lesson focused on practical engineering reasoning.
