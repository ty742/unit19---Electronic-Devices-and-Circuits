Title: Engineering Challenge Brief – “Can You Make It Work?”

============================================================
YOUR ROLE
============================================================

You are joining an electronics engineering team as a:

JUNIOR ELECTRONICS TECHNICIAN

Your team works on the development and testing of electronic
modules used in real products.

Today you have been given a prototype circuit that has failed
its production test.

Your supervisor has asked you to investigate the problem.

Your instruction is simple:

                 “CAN YOU MAKE IT WORK?”

============================================================
THE ENGINEERING PROBLEM
============================================================

A prototype electronic circuit has been assembled on a breadboard.

The circuit should perform a simple function when the power supply
is connected.

However:

IT DOES NOT WORK AS EXPECTED.

You have not been told what is wrong.

Your job is to:

1. Work safely.
2. Understand what the circuit is supposed to do.
3. Inspect the circuit.
4. Predict possible causes.
5. Carry out appropriate tests.
6. Identify the fault.
7. Correct the fault.
8. Test the circuit again.
9. Prove that the repair has worked.
10. Record what you discovered.

============================================================
IMPORTANT ENGINEERING RULE
============================================================

DO NOT GUESS.

DO NOT RANDOMLY REPLACE COMPONENTS.

DO NOT POWER A CIRCUIT UNTIL YOU ARE SATISFIED THAT IT IS SAFE.

A professional engineer uses evidence to make decisions.

Your troubleshooting process is:

OBSERVE
   ↓
PREDICT
   ↓
TEST
   ↓
LOCATE
   ↓
FIX
   ↓
VERIFY

============================================================
YOUR MISSION
============================================================

Complete the following stages.

STAGE 1 – SAFETY

Before touching the circuit:

- Identify possible hazards.
- Identify the correct isolation procedure.
- Check the bench and equipment.
- Confirm the power supply is OFF.
- Follow your tutor's laboratory safety instructions.

STAGE 2 – UNDERSTAND

Study the circuit schematic.

Identify:

- Power supply
- 0 V/ground
- Resistors
- LED
- Switch
- Jumper connections
- Expected current path

Ask yourself:

“What should happen when this circuit is working?”

STAGE 3 – INSPECT

Before applying power:

- Check component orientation.
- Check component values.
- Check wiring.
- Check polarity.
- Check breadboard connections.
- Check for unintended shorts.
- Check the power-supply setting.

STAGE 4 – PREDICT

Write down:

“What do I think is wrong?”

Give a reason.

STAGE 5 – TEST

Choose a measurement that could support or reject your idea.

For every test, record:

WHAT I TESTED:
____________________________________________________________

WHY I TESTED IT:
____________________________________________________________

WHAT I EXPECTED:
____________________________________________________________

WHAT I MEASURED:
____________________________________________________________

WHAT THE RESULT TELLS ME:
____________________________________________________________

STAGE 6 – LOCATE

Use your evidence to identify the most likely fault.

Do not change anything until you can explain why you are changing it.

STAGE 7 – FIX

Make the appropriate correction.

Record exactly what you changed.

STAGE 8 – VERIFY

Test the circuit again.

You must be able to prove that it now works.

Answer:

- What did you test?
- What happened?
- How do you know the repair was successful?

============================================================
THE ENGINEERING CHALLENGE
============================================================

Your team will be judged on more than whether the LED eventually
lights.

You will be successful if you can demonstrate:

[ ] Safe working

[ ] Correct schematic interpretation

[ ] Good pre-power checking

[ ] Appropriate use of test equipment

[ ] Logical troubleshooting

[ ] Evidence-based decision making

[ ] Correct repair

[ ] Verification of the repair

[ ] Clear technical documentation

============================================================
TEAMWORK
============================================================

Work in pairs.

Suggested roles:

BUILDER
- Checks the physical circuit.
- Makes approved changes.
- Carries out construction tasks.

TESTER
- Performs measurements.
- Operates test equipment.
- Records test results.

RECORDER
- Records observations and decisions.
- Maintains the investigation record.

If you are working in pairs, combine the Recorder role with
Builder or Tester as agreed by your tutor.

Rotate roles where possible.

CHALLENGE LEVELS
============================================================

CORE CHALLENGE

Find and repair the fault using the schematic and troubleshooting
process.

STRETCH CHALLENGE 1

Your circuit contains two faults.

Can you identify both?

STRETCH CHALLENGE 2

The circuit has an intermittent fault.

Can you identify the likely cause?

STRETCH CHALLENGE 3

You are given a circuit without a schematic.

Can you determine enough about the circuit to diagnose the problem?

STRETCH CHALLENGE 4

Write a troubleshooting procedure that another technician could
follow to diagnose the same fault.
