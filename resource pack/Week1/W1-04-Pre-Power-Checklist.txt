Title: Pre-Power Safety Checklist

============================================================
YOUR ROLE
============================================================

You are about to power a prototype electronic circuit.

Your responsibility is to make sure the circuit is safe and ready
for testing before connecting the power supply.

DO NOT POWER THE CIRCUIT UNTIL THE CHECKLIST HAS BEEN COMPLETED.

============================================================
1. WORK AREA CHECK
============================================================

[ ] Work area is clean and organised.

[ ] Unnecessary equipment has been removed.

[ ] Wires are arranged so they cannot easily be pulled out.

[ ] No damaged equipment is being used.

[ ] There are no loose metal objects near the circuit.

[ ] I know where the electrical isolation/emergency stop is.

[ ] I know what to do in an emergency.

============================================================
2. POWER SUPPLY CHECK
============================================================

[ ] The correct power supply has been selected.

[ ] The supply is switched OFF before connecting the circuit.

[ ] The supply voltage has been checked.

Required supply voltage:

____________________ V

Actual supply setting:

____________________ V

[ ] The supply current limit has been set appropriately where
    applicable.

[ ] The power leads are connected to the correct terminals.

[ ] Polarity has been checked.

============================================================
3. CIRCUIT CHECK
============================================================

[ ] The physical circuit matches the schematic.

[ ] Components are in the correct positions.

[ ] Component values have been checked.

[ ] Polarity-sensitive components have been checked.

Examples:

- LED
- Diode
- Electrolytic capacitor
- Transistor
- IC/op-amp

[ ] Resistor values have been checked.

[ ] Jumper wires are correctly positioned.

[ ] Breadboard connections have been checked.

[ ] There are no unintended shorts.

[ ] Components are seated correctly.

6. MULTIMETER CHECK
============================================================

Before connecting the multimeter:

[ ] I know what I am measuring.

[ ] I know whether I need voltage, current, resistance or continuity.

[ ] I have selected the correct meter function.

[ ] The leads are connected to the correct meter terminals.

[ ] I know where the probes will be placed.

[ ] I know approximately what reading I expect.

IMPORTANT:

Never place an ammeter directly across a voltage supply.

============================================================
8. FINAL SAFETY STOP
============================================================

Before requesting permission to power the circuit, confirm:

[ ] I understand the circuit.

[ ] I understand the expected behaviour.

[ ] I have checked the supply.

[ ] I have checked polarity.

[ ] I have checked component values.

[ ] I have checked the wiring.

[ ] I have checked for shorts.

[ ] I have selected the correct meter settings.

[ ] I know what I will measure.

[ ] I know how to isolate the supply.

[ ] I am confident that the circuit is ready to test.

============================================================
9. STOP / CHECK / ASK
============================================================

If you are unsure about any part of the circuit:

STOP.

Do not guess.

Do not power the circuit.

Ask your tutor.

A professional engineer would rather delay a test than damage
equipment, components or people.
