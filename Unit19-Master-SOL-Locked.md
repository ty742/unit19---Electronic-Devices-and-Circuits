# Unit 19 — Master Scheme of Learning

**Delivery:** 3 hours per week, including 20-minute break  
**Learning Aim A:** 7 weeks  
**Learning Aim B:** 9 weeks  
**Learning Aim C:** 2 weeks  
**Total:** 18 taught sessions  
**Half-term and Christmas holidays excluded**

| Week/date | Aim of the lesson | Learning to come from the activities: What should learners know and what can they demonstrate? | Planned Activities (including stretch & challenge and strategies that will be used to support all learners including SEND learners) | Assessment of learning | Embedded learning |

| **A1 – 7 Sep 2026** | **Unit 19 Launch – “Can You Make It Work?”** Introduce safe electronic working and the engineering troubleshooting process. | Identify common electronic components; interpret a simple circuit schematic; recognise electrical and workshop hazards; explain basic isolation and emergency procedures; carry out pre-power checks; safely use a low-voltage PSU and multimeter; apply a systematic troubleshooting process. | **Engineering Toolbox:** baseline retrieval of basic electrical quantities, component identification and safe working. **Workplace scenario:** “You are a junior electronics technician. A prototype board has failed production test. Your supervisor wants you to find out why.” Students first complete simulation and build of the basic LED circuit used in week 1 of unit 6. They measure voltage and current, confirm correct operation and LED lights up on the breadboard, NOT connected to the Arduino in any way. Then introduce a fault and ask Students to inspect this deliberately faulty circuit, identify components from the schematic, complete a safety/risk check and perform pre-power checks. They follow **Observe → Predict → Test → Locate → Fix → Verify** to diagnose the fault. Introduce Builder/Tester roles and the **Build → Test → Break → Fix → Repeat** culture. **Stretch:** locate two faults or investigate an intermittent fault. **SEND:** visual safety prompts, colour-coded schematic, component photographs, breadboard map, vocabulary bank, checklist and paired roles. **Catch-up:** absent students complete the online safety/troubleshooting module and guided simulation before a supervised practical. | **PoE A01 – Prototype Fault Investigation:** writes a report of how they fixed the circuit, completed safety checklist, annotated schematic, measurements, photograph, fault diagnosis and repair record. Individual questioning checks safe isolation and reasoning. | Safe working, H&S, emergency procedures, technical vocabulary, electrical quantities, digital evidence organisation, teamwork, resilience, structured problem solving and sustainable repair rather than unnecessary replacement. |
| **A2 – 14 Sep 2026** | **Diode Investigation – “Protect and Control the Power”** Investigate diode behaviour and apply it to a practical power-electronics problem. | Identify relevant diode types; explain forward and reverse bias; predict diode behaviour; interpret basic diode characteristics; select an appropriate component; construct, simulate and test a diode circuit. | **Engineering Toolbox:** retrieval of Week 1 safety, polarity, voltage/current and multimeter use. **Workplace scenario:** “Your design team needs a small input/power stage that safely controls or rectifies voltage. Which diode circuit should we use?” Students identify diode polarity, investigate forward/reverse behaviour, predict measurements, simulate the circuit, construct it on breadboard and compare measured and simulated results. Investigate a practical application such as rectification or Zener regulation. Introduce datasheet selection by comparing real diode ratings. Students transfer their findings into the diode section of the assignment. **Stretch:** compare two real components and justify selection using voltage, current and power ratings. **SEND:** polarity card, guided schematic, component-selection chart, worked calculation, preconfigured simulation, structured measurement table and sentence starters. | **PoE A02 – Diode Engineering Investigation:** schematic, simulation evidence, physical measurements, calculations, component information and engineering conclusion. | Ohm’s law, power, units, graphs, datasheets, simulation, component selection, technical explanation, safe current limiting, teamwork and sustainability through appropriate component selection. |
| **A3 – 21 Sep 2026** | **Transistor Investigation – “Control a Real Load”** Use transistor devices to solve a practical switching/control problem and investigate amplification. | Identify transistor types and terminals; explain cut-off, active and saturation regions; construct a transistor switch; obtain appropriate measurements; calculate basic current gain; relate transistor behaviour to practical applications. | **Engineering Toolbox:** retrieval of diode behaviour, polarity, power and troubleshooting. **Workplace scenario:** “A controller can provide only a small signal, but the product needs to control a larger load. Design an interface.” Students identify transistor terminals from datasheets, build a transistor switching circuit controlling an LED/load, predict base/collector behaviour and measure voltages/currents. They then investigate a simple common-emitter amplifier and compare simulation with physical results. Tutor deliberately introduces a wiring/component fault and students diagnose it from measurements. Students develop the transistor section of their assignment immediately. **Stretch:** investigate the effect of base resistance or load resistance on operation. **SEND:** transistor pinout card, colour-coded terminals, worked gain example, preconfigured simulation, measurement scaffold and clearly defined practical roles. | **PoE A03 – Transistor Engineering Investigation:** schematic, simulation, physical measurements, gain calculation and fault-finding record. Tutor questioning probes why the transistor is operating in the observed region. | Current gain, ratios, technical explanations, datasheet interpretation, simulation, file management, fault diagnosis, component ratings, teamwork, perseverance and confidence. |
| **A4 – 28 Sep 2026** | **Op-Amp Investigation – “Condition the Sensor Signal”** Apply operational amplifiers to signal conditioning, amplification and frequency-response problems. | Explain the purpose of negative feedback; construct an op-amp circuit; calculate voltage gain; measure input/output waveforms; investigate frequency response; determine cut-off frequency. | **Engineering Toolbox:** cumulative retrieval of diode/transistor behaviour, gain and measurement techniques. **Workplace scenario:** “A sensor produces a small signal that the control system cannot use directly. Design a signal-conditioning stage.” Students begin with a comparator challenge before constructing an inverting/non-inverting amplifier. They predict gain and waveform behaviour, simulate the circuit, build it and use a multimeter, oscilloscope and function generator to investigate actual performance. Finish with **“Find the Frequency!”**, where students investigate an op-amp filter and determine its cut-off frequency. Students develop the op-amp section of their assignment from their own practical results. **Stretch:** explain differences between theoretical, simulated and measured cut-off frequency. **SEND:** annotated op-amp diagram, gain scaffold, oscilloscope setup card, sample waveform, guided frequency table and paired support. | **PoE A04 – Signal Conditioning Investigation:** schematic, simulation, oscilloscope capture, gain calculation, frequency-response data and technical explanation. | Gain, frequency, graphs, cut-off calculations, oscilloscope skills, instrumentation, technical communication, safe test-equipment connections and collaborative problem solving. |
| **A5 – 5 Oct 2026** | **Prototype Verification – “Prove the Design Works”** Verify the three analogue investigations against technical requirements and strengthen the evidence portfolio. | Review circuits against a specification; identify missing or unreliable evidence; repeat measurements where necessary; organise technical evidence; explain whether each circuit meets its intended function. | **Engineering Toolbox:** cumulative retrieval of analogue components, calculations, measurement and troubleshooting. **Workplace scenario:** “Your prototype is ready for design verification. You must provide evidence that it works before another engineer can sign it off.” Students conduct an **Engineering Evidence Audit** using **Requirement → Schematic → Simulation → Build → Test → Results → Explanation**. Students re-test weak circuits rather than simply rewriting reports. Tutor runs targeted mini-clinics based on evidence gaps. **Stretch:** justify component choices from datasheets and assess measurement reliability. **SEND:** evidence checklist, exemplar evidence, staged sign-offs, circuit-selection options, report scaffold and targeted intervention. | **PoE A05 – Prototype Verification Record.** Tutor checks progress against the formal assessment requirements and identifies missing or weak evidence. Students correct gaps during the session. | Assessment literacy, technical reporting, mathematical checking, evidence management, quality assurance, specifications, deadlines, safe independent testing, planning and organisation. |
| **A6 – 12 Oct 2026** | **Test Engineering – “Why Doesn’t Reality Match Simulation?”** Analyse differences between theoretical, simulated and physical results. | Calculate relevant quantities and errors; compare theoretical, simulated and measured results; identify sources of variation; explain discrepancies using evidence; judge the reliability of measurements. | **Engineering Toolbox:** cumulative retrieval of analogue circuits, gain, frequency, power and measurement. **Workplace scenario:** “Simulation predicted 5.00 V; the prototype gives 4.63 V. The design review is tomorrow. Can you explain the discrepancy?” Students investigate component tolerance, device variation, instrument accuracy, loading, supply variation, breadboard effects and practical device characteristics. They apply the investigation to their own circuits and create comparison tables/graphs. Conduct a **“How do you know?” peer design review**, where students challenge the evidence behind one another’s conclusions. **Stretch:** rank likely causes and justify the ranking. **SEND:** worked example, graph scaffold, error prompt cards, calculation template and sentence starters. | **PoE A06 – Engineering Analysis:** theory/simulation/test comparison, calculations, graphs and evidence-backed explanations. | Percentage error, tolerance, averages, graphs, significant figures, analytical writing, spreadsheets, QA, continuous improvement, evidence-based diagnosis and sustainability. |
| **A7 – 19 Oct 2026** | **Design Review – “Would You Put This Circuit Into a Product?”** Evaluate the analogue investigations and complete Learning Aim A evidence. | Evaluate circuit performance; compare simulation and measured results; identify limitations; recommend improvements; communicate a technically justified conclusion. | **Engineering Toolbox:** cumulative retrieval of Learning Aim A. **Workplace scenario:** “You are presenting the prototype to the senior design engineer. Would you approve it for the next development stage?” Students undertake a final practical verification using **Read → Check → Test → Diagnose → Explain**. **Simulation vs Reality Gallery Walk:** students compare selected results and discuss causes of variation. They complete final evaluations and identify what would need changing before production. **Stretch:** propose and justify a technical redesign. **SEND:** individual evidence tracker, oral rehearsal, structured evaluation prompts, model conclusion and tutor conference. | **PoE A07 – Final Design Review.** Learning Aim A summative evidence is completed from the accumulated portfolio. Practical demonstration and individual questioning support verification of understanding. | Design review, presentation, technical evaluation, documentation, deadlines, safe independent practice, professional judgement, confidence, accountability and constructive technical critique. |
| **26 Oct 2026** | **HALF TERM – NO SESSION** | — | No taught session. Online catch-up resources remain available for students who need to recover outstanding evidence. | Progress monitoring only. | Independent organisation and responsibility. |
| **B1 – 2 Nov 2026** | **Digital Launch – “Can You Build the Decision?”** Introduce digital electronics through real control-system problems. | Identify AND, OR, NOT, NAND, NOR and XOR gates; interpret symbols; explain binary logic states; construct truth tables; recognise TTL/CMOS characteristics and safely test simple logic circuits. | **Engineering Toolbox:** cumulative retrieval from Learning Aim A. **Workplace scenario:** “A machine needs to decide whether it is safe to start. Several sensor conditions must be satisfied.” Students physically investigate gates, predict outputs, build circuits and test them. **Logic Gate Challenge:** **predict → wire → test → explain**. Introduce TTL/CMOS and logic voltage levels through practical measurements. **Stretch:** determine an unknown gate from observed inputs/outputs. **SEND:** gate-symbol cards, truth-table scaffold, colour-coded logic states, pre-built examples and paired roles. | **PoE B01 – Logic Gate Investigation:** truth tables, schematics, photographs, measurements, simulation evidence and explanation. Individual questioning verifies understanding. | Digital literacy, Boolean notation, binary concepts, measurement, teamwork, safe breadboarding, technical vocabulary and problem solving. |
| **B2 – 9 Nov 2026** | **Combinational Logic – “Design the Control System”** Translate a real control requirement into a working logic circuit. | Translate a written requirement into a truth table; derive a Boolean expression; construct a combinational circuit; simulate and physically test it; verify operation against the specification. | **Engineering Toolbox:** retrieval of logic symbols, truth tables and analogue measurement skills. **Workplace scenario:** “A machine has three sensors. The motor should operate only when the correct combination of conditions exists.” Students convert the requirement into a truth table, derive the logic expression, construct the circuit, simulate it and test every relevant input combination. A deliberate wiring fault is introduced and students use the truth table and measurements to locate it. **Stretch:** add an additional safety condition. **SEND:** partially completed truth table, colour-coded logic paths, Boolean-expression scaffold, prepared simulation and paired Builder/Tester roles. | **PoE B02 – Combinational Control System:** requirement interpretation, truth table, Boolean expression, schematic, simulation and physical test evidence. | Boolean logic, specification interpretation, systematic testing, technical documentation, fault finding, teamwork and professional communication. |
| **B3 – 16 Nov 2026** | **Boolean Algebra – “Can You Make It Simpler?”** Use Boolean algebra to optimise an existing digital circuit. | Apply Boolean laws and De Morgan’s theorem; simplify Boolean expressions; redesign a circuit with fewer gates; verify that the simplified circuit performs the same function. | **Engineering Toolbox:** retrieval of gates, truth tables and combinational logic. **Workplace scenario:** “Your circuit works, but the production engineer says it uses too many gates. Can you reduce its complexity without changing its function?” Students first build/test an intentionally inefficient circuit. | **B4 – 23 Nov 2026** | **Karnaugh Maps – “Find the Shortest Route”** Use Karnaugh maps to minimise digital logic. | Construct and interpret K-maps; group terms correctly; derive simplified Boolean expressions; implement and verify a minimal logic circuit. | **Engineering Toolbox:** retrieval of Boolean laws and circuit optimisation. **Workplace scenario:** “The production team wants the smallest practical implementation of this control system. Can you find the shortest route from inputs to output?” Students convert a specification into a truth table and K-map, identify valid groups, derive the simplified expression and construct the reduced circuit. **Gate Count Challenge:** teams compete for **fewest gates + correct operation + cleanest design**. Students verify the optimised circuit through simulation and physical testing. **SEND:** K-map template, grouping prompts, worked example, colour-coded cells and guided peer support. | **PoE B04 – Optimisation Challenge:** truth table, K-map, simplified expression, schematic, simulation and physical verification. | Mathematical reasoning, logical thinking, optimisation, teamwork, communication, engineering efficiency and confidence in solving unfamiliar problems. |

## Attendance and Catch-Up Model

This is an integral part of the SOL.

For every taught week:

**Engineering Toolbox → Main lesson**

For students who were absent:

**What you missed → Essential learning → Retrieval → Guided simulation → Catch-up practical → Evidence → Progress check**

### Three levels of response

**One missed session:**  
Self-directed catch-up + supervised practical/evidence verification.

**Two missed sessions:**  
Structured recovery pathway + targeted tutor intervention.

**Significant absence:**  
Individual recovery plan identifying essential knowledge, practical work and outstanding evidence.

The principle is:

> **Students catch themselves up wherever possible; the tutor intervenes where learning, practical competence or authentic assessment evidence requires it.**

## Retrieval Model

Every lesson contains:

### Engineering Toolbox
Short cumulative retrieval from previous learning.

### Predict → Test → Explain
Students predict what a circuit should do before testing it.

### Exit Retrieval
Students retrieve key learning before leaving.

### Cumulative retrieval
Earlier Learning Aim A knowledge continues appearing during Learning Aim B.

The unit therefore deliberately avoids:

> **Finish A → forget A → start B**

and instead uses:

> **A → retrieve A → build B on A → retrieve A+B → build C on A+B**

## Master Design Principle

The unit is designed around:

> **Workplace problem → Retrieve → Predict → Simulate → Build → Measure → Break → Fix → Analyse → Document → Reflect**

rather than:

> **Teach theory → give assignment → students write report.**

The **Portfolio of Evidence (PoE)** is the continuous thread through the unit. Students progressively build authentic evidence from practical investigations, analysis, troubleshooting and professional reviews. This evidence is then used to support and complete the summative assessment requirements.

All subsequent detailed lesson plans, student activities, support packs, simulations, formative assessments, PoE documents and interactive website resources should be generated from this master SOL without changing the underlying weekly sequence unless the master is explicitly revised.
